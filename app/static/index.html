About

The checker compares an original legal sentence with a simplified version and estimates how much of the legal meaning is kept, on a scale from 1 to 10.

Method

  1. Both texts are encoded together by a fine-tuned multilingual transformer (XLM-RoBERTa base).
  2. Three output heads give the score, a decision (preserved ≥ 6.5, not preserved ≤ 2, otherwise partially preserved) and nine legal error types.
  3. A rule-based comparison checks obligations and permissions, negation, exceptions, conditions, numbers and dates, scope words and the parties.
  4. Integrated Gradients shows which words lowered or raised the score.

Data

FrJUDGE (Beauchemin et al., EMNLP 2025): 297 French insurance clauses with simplifications, each scored by five law students. The pairs were translated into English and Hindi with NLLB-200, and extended with identical, unrelated and rule-edited pairs.

Limitations

  • English and Hindi scores were learned from translated French judgments.
  • The model is weakest on changed numbers and dates.
  • Inputs longer than 256 sub-word tokens are truncated.
  • This is a research tool, not legal advice.
